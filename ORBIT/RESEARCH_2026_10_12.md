# Research review and next method for audio prompted LiDAR segmentation

## Measured diagnosis

The full Sonata validation benchmark is 768 observations from nine scenes. The
observation-only candidate system reaches 33.66% mean foreground IoU; the
predicted-candidate reranker reaches 33.79% in its saved bank and 33.63% when
rerun end to end. Comparing individual records found 25/768 changed selected
IoUs and 24/768 changed selected proposal identities across the two GPU runs.
Floating-point perturbations around nearly tied candidate seeds are a likely
cause, although this is not yet proven for every disagreement. A deterministic
relative-score quantization and voxel-index tie break has been added to seed
ranking. Re-evaluating all 768 observations on both RTX 5090 and 6000 Ada
gave 33.6249% and 33.6274% IoU respectively, with only one selected-proposal
identity difference (down from 24) and nine small mask-IoU differences. The
largest remaining sample difference was 2.75 IoU points. The best
available candidate per observation has
65.27% IoU **only when labels select it**. Source-point seed recall is 90.49%.
Therefore seed discovery is relatively strong, while complete-mask quality and
object choice remain major errors. The oracle is a ceiling only for *the current
candidate set*, not for the task. Reaching 60% with this candidate set would
require recovering about 92% of its IoU oracle, whereas the current selector
recovers about 52%. Reaching 70–80% requires better candidates too.

The saved training bank has mean best-candidate IoU 75.12% and selected IoU
52.51%; the separate validation bank has 65.27% and 33.68%. In 164 of 768
validation observations a candidate reaches at least 50% IoU but the selected
mask is below 10%. This large gap
indicates scene transfer is a serious failure mode. Training on 34 scenes and
selecting checkpoints on nine validation scenes means repeated model choices
against these same nine scenes are increasingly optimistic. Audio has only two
independent dry clips per category; spatialized recordings multiply samples,
not independent acoustic events. The V2 array is a calibrated four-microphone
tetrahedron. Its directional posterior is informative, but a single receiver
pose cannot by itself determine source range, and room reflections create
false direction modes. These constraints make any promised 60–80% unsupported.
The largest selection regrets by category are clock (51.4 percentage points),
refrigerator (49.5), TV (43.6), and speaker (42.0); see the exact scene and
category breakdown in `evaluation/sonata_failure_analysis.json`. Hard observations
have 36.0 points of selection regret, above easy observations at 29.5 points.

## Closest relevant work and implications

* [Mask3D](https://github.com/JonasSchult/Mask3D) predicts all 3D instances
  with learned queries and direct mask losses. Its scene-level proposal design
  is the most relevant geometry alternative to seed-by-seed masks. Published
  checkpoints expect benchmark-specific inputs, so direct use on XYZ-only
  tetrahedral LiDAR needs evaluation and likely adaptation.
* [SOLE, ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/f53437debdd397c42929d929614bc705-Abstract-Conference.html)
  shows that semantic information inside the 3D mask generator matters. An
  audio-category-conditioned query or mask decoder is a better next model than
  merely adding an audio classifier after geometric proposals.
* [AQFormer, IJCAI 2023](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0097.pdf)
  conditions object queries on audio for image-based sounding-object masks.
  [A CVPR 2025 vision-centric decoder](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Huang_Revisiting_Audio-Visual_Segmentation_with_Vision-Centric_Transformer_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)
  instead passes sound into region-derived queries. Both support adapting a
  3D instance query to the sound rather than relying only on a late selector.
  Neither architecture is directly reusable without replacing its image stream.
* [Audio-Visual Instance Segmentation, CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Guo_Audio-Visual_Instance_Segmentation_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
  explicitly studies individual sounding-object instances, reinforcing that
  category-only sound detection is insufficient when multiple objects or
  instances are present. Its video input prevents direct checkpoint reuse here.
* [PointSeg, ICCV 2025 workshop](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025W/E2E3D/html/He_PointSeg_A_Training-Free_Paradigm_for_3D_Scene_Segmentation_via_Foundation_ICCVW_2025_paper.html)
  reports strong 3D instance proposals from visual foundation models. Its
  rendered-image/SAM path is worth testing only if synthetic views genuinely
  preserve the XYZ geometry; it is not an immediate XYZ-only replacement.
* [Utonia](https://github.com/Pointcept/Utonia) explicitly supports absent
  color and normal modalities using zeros. Its official frozen encoder is
  already extracted for this project and its downstream models are training.
  Its point features improve the perfect-instance source selector diagnostic
  from 64.06% to 75.78%. In the completed point-head ablation, Utonia XYZ
  features reached 33.12% full-validation IoU versus 27.69% for the longer
  Sonata point-head run, a gain of 5.43 points (scene-bootstrap descriptive
  interval +1.82 to +8.73 points). The completed combined object-mask score
  is reported below.
* [SoundLoc3D, WACV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/html/He_SoundLoc3D_Invisible_3D_Sound_Source_Localization_and_Classification_using_a_WACV_2025_paper.html)
  fuses array sound with physical surface cues. Its RGB-D multiview observations
  are not available here, but its geometric acoustic association motivates
  scoring whole 3D objects by the measured direction likelihood.
* [Object-aware Sound Source Localization, CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Um_Object-aware_Sound_Source_Localization_via_Audio-Visual_Scene_Understanding_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
  supports reasoning about which physical object can make the heard event.
  Its image and MLLM parts cannot be assumed to transfer to XYZ-only LiDAR.
* [3D Audio-Visual Segmentation / EchoSegNet](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=DBZbTztEz0)
  uses audio-informed spatial refinement to retain the sounding 3D instance.
  It needs visual frames and 3D Gaussian splats, so it is methodological
  evidence for instance-level refinement, not an immediately usable checkpoint.
* [LAION CLAP](https://github.com/LAION-AI/CLAP) supplies audio-text features
  already used here. The current frozen text prior and light residual is safer
  than full CLAP fine-tuning given the few independent dry clips. More audio
  data or new independent events would change that recommendation.
* [BEATs official checkpoints](https://github.com/microsoft/unilm/blob/master/beats/README.md)
  offer AudioSet-supervised event features as a complementary audio encoder.
  They are a plausible way to reduce CLAP's audio-identity errors, but the
  AudioSet event ontology must be mapped to the *heard* dry clips, not simply
  to object names. TV static and speaker feedback are concrete cases where
  a naive object-label map would be wrong. This is a proposed experiment,
  not a measured gain on V2.
* [FSD50K](https://zenodo.org/records/4060432) offers independent annotated
  sound events and its [vocabulary](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Fhrozen/FSD50k/blob/main/labels/vocabulary.csv)
  explicitly includes clock, keyboard, door, mechanical fan, microwave oven,
  printer, and toilet flush. It does not cover every object name in V2; event
  to object mapping must be audited clip by clip. A separate external-audio
  experiment could improve semantic robustness, but adding it without that
  mapping would introduce label noise and licensing obligations.
* [Acoustic source-distance work](https://signalprocessingsociety.org/publications-resources/ieee-transactions-audio-speech-and-language-processing/2020/02/binaural)
  uses direct-to-reverberant energy as a range cue. If different objects lie
  along the same acoustic direction, a range estimate could resolve them.
  This dataset's dry recordings differ greatly in loudness, so absolute RMS
  alone is unlikely to transfer; range should be tested with training-scene
  supervision and a held-out scene evaluation before adding it to ranking.
  That probe was run: frozen CLAP features predicted source range at 1.02 m
  validation MAE against a 1.30 m constant baseline. Scene-cross-fitted TRAIN
  distances were then used to calibrate a candidate-center distance penalty.
  Full validation foreground IoU changed only from 33.684% to 33.698% on the
  saved Sonata candidate bank, which is negligible; this scoring rule is not
  adopted. It does not rule out better direct-to-reverberant features.

The completed learned-spatial-only Sonata point-head ablation obtained 32.59%
full-validation foreground IoU, versus 27.69% for the long Sonata point-head
baseline (+4.90 points, descriptive paired scene-bootstrap interval +0.46 to
+8.73 points). This is below the 33.66% object-mask system, so better direction
estimation by itself does not close the object-selection gap.

A controlled within-scan audio intervention is possible in V2: 147 validation
groups contain 415 observations and 454 distinct-source pairs with exactly the
same XYZ point array and listener pose. For the stable Sonata hybrid, 89.65% of
these pairs select different proposal origins after changing the sound, but
only 9.47% have both sounding-object masks at IoU >= 0.5. Thus audio affects
predictions, yet reliable discrimination between two possible sounding objects
in the *same geometric scene* remains poor. These pairwise percentages are
descriptive and not an independent benchmark score; see
`evaluation/same_pose_val.json` and `evaluation/sonata_same_pose_detailed_val.json`.
The 53 same-category pairs have a similar 9.43% both-correct rate, so a category
classifier alone cannot solve this controlled subset. The pairs also
provide well-controlled contrastive examples for training a stronger
audio-object alignment head. Only one pair retained an identical geometric
prompt; another 46 pairs selected the audio-conditioned APEX fallback in both
conditions, for which identical origin does not imply identical masks.
The aligned Utonia training cache contains only 14 same-pose, distinct-source
groups with exactly identical voxel XYZ, versus 147 on validation. This sharp
imbalance limits contrastive training on genuinely identical scans. The
pair-trained query uses only those 14 TRAIN groups; its 74.23% perfect-prompt
pair accuracy on validation is a privileged diagnostic, not a deployable score.
The raw-XYZ training-wide audit was deferred because it competed with GPU
cache I/O; the cache-level finding is verified by exact array hashing and
equality. An expanded training render set with several sounding sources at
each fixed listener pose is a high-priority data intervention.

The completed frozen-checkpoint observation-only evaluation of the pair-trained
Utonia query reaches **45.89% foreground IoU** on all 768 validation
observations (easy 47.17%, medium 45.66%, hard 41.46%). The point-head
checkpoint was selected at epoch 10 and remained best through the full
24-epoch schedule. The matched Utonia hybrid scores 41.69%; the
pair query adds 4.19 points (descriptive paired scene-bootstrap interval
+2.65 to +5.75). Against the stable Sonata hybrid at 33.62%, the paired
gain is 12.26 points (+8.31 to +15.18 descriptive interval). The best mask
per example in the *same generated bank* averages 71.41% IoU only when
ground-truth labels select it. This diagnostic leaves a 25.52-point selection
gap and is not a deployable score. On 454 shared-XYZ, different-source validation pairs, the
pair-query method raises the both-IoU-at-least-0.5 rate from the matched
Utonia hybrid's 27.09% to 33.26%; the 53 same-category pairs rise from
15.09% to 20.75%. These paired subsets are descriptive and overlapping.
Category analysis localizes two different bottlenecks: keyboard reaches only
30.02% selected IoU and 37.18% best-candidate IoU, with a median of 88.5
visible target points on validation; finer point-level geometry or targeted
small-object mask completion is needed. Door reaches 25.87% selected IoU
despite 66.05% candidate coverage and a median 1,852 visible target points;
its principal problem is choosing the door mask, not lack of points. Speaker
and clock also have large selection gaps. This argues against treating every
weak class with the same backbone change.
An independently trained audio query using all 3,264 TRAIN observations now
scores **46.63%** full-validation foreground IoU (easy 49.02%, medium
44.68%, hard 41.69%). Its matched no-query Utonia system scores 41.70%, a
+4.93-point gain (descriptive scene bootstrap +3.74 to +6.05). Against the
stable Sonata score of 33.62%, the gain is +13.00 points (+9.77 to +15.27
descriptive). The base query is +0.74 points over the pair-trained query, but
the paired scene interval crosses zero (-0.20 to +1.77). This choice is
validation-selected, not independent evidence of generalization. On the same
454 fixed-XYZ, different-source validation pairs, both masks reach at least
0.5 IoU for 34.36%, while the 53 same-category pairs reach 15.09%; the
pair-trained query remains better on that same-category subset (20.75%).
Shuffling the entire audio observation across scenes reduces the base-query
score to 2.33%; replacing learned directional evidence with uniform spatial
evidence reduces it to 24.13%. Raw XYZ/four-channel WAV and cached inference
produce identical selected original-point masks on a checked observation.
These interventions demonstrate modality use, not that the model has solved
source-object grounding across all scenes.
The completed 24-epoch Utonia plus learned-spatial direct point head retained
its epoch-10 checkpoint at 37.32% full-validation IoU. Its own full-validation
controls fell to 1.18% when audio embeddings and spatial posteriors were
shuffled across scenes, and to 11.91% with uniform spatial evidence. These are
input interventions, not alternative held-out scores; they show the direct head
uses the observed sound and array geometry rather than silently relying only
on LiDAR.

## Frozen held-out result and remaining gap

The two audio-query scores were summed with fixed weights of 1.0 each; this
was the best full-validation rule at **47.10%** mean foreground IoU (easy
49.33%, medium 46.00%, hard 40.94%). The half-weight pair-query alternative
scored 46.60%, while the base query alone scored 46.63%. The 0.47-point gain
over the base query has a descriptive paired scene interval of -0.50 to
+1.53 points and should not be treated as strong evidence of superiority.
The method, checkpoint hashes, weights, and TEST feature paths were frozen in
`evaluation/final_method_frozen.json` before one held-out TEST evaluation.
Post-freeze full-VAL controls lowered this final ensemble from 47.10% to
2.67% under cross-scene audio shuffling and to 24.34% when learned spatial
evidence was replaced by a uniform directional posterior. They verify
modality dependence but did not change the frozen model.

That one evaluation scored **37.77%** mean foreground IoU on all 768 TEST
observations from nine scenes disjoint from TRAIN and VAL, with easy/medium/
hard at 38.32/37.90/35.06%. Its matched no-query baseline scored 32.52%.
The audio-query uplift was +5.26 points on TEST versus +5.40 on VAL; all nine
TEST scenes improved over their matched baseline. A scene-cluster bootstrap
gives a descriptive +3.56 to +6.88-point interval for the TEST uplift. This
does not establish superiority over external methods, which were not tested
under this exact protocol.

The TEST best-candidate oracle is 63.44% and uses labels to choose a proposal;
it is **not** a deployable score. It fell 7.97 points from VAL's 71.41%, while
the final selected score fell 9.33 points. The remaining TEST proposal-choice
gap is 25.67 points. Thus the audio-query improvement generalized as a
relative gain, but the underlying instance proposer and choice both limit
absolute performance. TV is a strong selection counterexample (13.01%
selected, 75.9% best-candidate), while keyboard is a proposal failure as well
(13.77% selected, 30.2% best-candidate). The requested 60–80% is not reached;
70–80% is impossible with this fixed candidate bank even under privileged
choice. Reusing TEST labels to tune a new method would forfeit its held-out
status; future experiments need a fresh untouched external benchmark.

The next train-only research program should be gated by measurable milestones:

1. Render several different sounding sources at each fixed listener/XYZ pose
   in TRAIN scenes and acquire independently recorded sound clips, preserving
   scene-disjoint folds. The present 14 exact-XYZ TRAIN counterfactual groups
   are too few to learn reliable same-scene source discrimination.
2. Replace the seed-by-seed prompt bank with a 3D instance-query decoder
   trained on all visible TRAIN instances, using Utonia XYZ features and
   point-level mask/Dice losses. A Mask3D-style candidate set should first
   raise its scene-cross-validated oracle materially above the current 63.44%
   held-out diagnostic; a 70–80% deployed score is impossible with the
   current TEST candidate set even under label-guided selection.
3. Train audio-conditioned queries to select among these new instances,
   emphasizing same-XYZ, different-source negatives and category-confusable
   events. Use TRAIN scene folds for architecture and threshold choices, not
   the already-used TEST. Add BEATs or CLAP adaptation only if a new-clip
   validation set shows event-identity errors; the relatively stable +5-point
   audio-query gain suggests proposal transfer is the first bottleneck.
4. Audit HM3D overlap in pretrained geometry weights and evaluate on fresh
   scenes and fresh audio recordings. Only then make publication-level
   generalization or superiority claims.

## Concrete next method: acoustic semantic instance queries

Represent each proposed whole-object mask as a query. Predict three independent
pieces of evidence from observation only: (1) 3D instance and category from
Utonia, (2) audio event category from CLAP, and (3) physically calibrated
microphone-array direction likelihood over the object's point directions.
Combine them in an audio-conditioned mask decoder and train with all valid
training instances, using sounding-instance targets for cross-modal selection.
Retain scene-disjoint training folds for choosing ranking weights and mask
thresholds; report full validation once per frozen candidate method. An
auxiliary ray-to-surface loss can teach the model to favor the physical object
intersecting the acoustic direction without requiring depth from the array.

The completed campaign found that changing the frozen geometry encoder,
adding learned spatial evidence, and training an audio-conditioned object
query each improved full VAL performance. The single-query shuffled-audio
control fell from 46.63% to 2.33%, while uniform spatial evidence yielded
24.13%. The final ensemble's corresponding controls are reported above.
An isolated semantic-aware selection probe pools per-point category scores
over each predicted mask and matches that distribution to sound.
The Sonata probe selected a 0.25 semantic weight from TRAIN candidates. Its
full validation score was 33.25%, below the unchanged 33.66%; simple late
semantic correction is therefore a negative result on this backbone.
A separate two-factor correction to the acoustic score and mask-size prior was
calibrated on 1,088 TRAIN prediction banks. A code audit found that the
original script used ranking-feature index 16 for acoustic evidence, although
the 12 geometry features put the intended acoustic value at index 28. The
original `evaluation/sonata_physics_calibrate_val.json` is retained as a historical
record and its acoustic-weight grid is invalid. After correcting the index and
reusing the same saved bank, TRAIN selected acoustic weight -0.5 and size
weight 0. The corrected full-validation score is 33.64%, versus 33.68% for the
unchanged baseline; see `evaluation/sonata_physics_calibrate_corrected_val.json`.
This does not support the correction on validation.
An audio-conditioned 3D query head is implemented in `orbit/audio_gate.py`.
It consumes frozen Utonia prompt-mask embeddings, predicted semantic scores,
CLAP waveform embeddings, and per-point directional evidence. It learns to
rank true sounding-object seeds above other instance and background seeds in
TRAIN scenes. The base and exact-XYZ pair-trained heads are now complete;
privileged true-seed accuracies remain separate from deployed segmentation.

Do not interpret perfect masks, correct point prompts, oracle candidate choice,
or repeated validation tuning as publishable test performance. Sonata and
Utonia have unresolved HM3D pretraining overlap with these scenes. Check this
before making unseen-scene or publication claims; ideally add a fully
independent scene and independently sourced sound-event evaluation set.
